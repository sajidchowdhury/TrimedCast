    32→    32→## Document Purpose
    33→    33→
    34→    34→This document specifies the complete forecasting engine for the TrimedCast platform — the mathematical models, the Python microservice architecture, API contracts, configuration, Bangladesh (BD)-specific seasonal logic, error metrics, and auto-recalibration triggers.
    35→    35→
    36→    36→TrimedCast is an **Integrated Seasonal Demand & Inventory Forecasting System** designed specifically for motorcycle parts businesses in Bangladesh, operating as a **multi-tenant SaaS platform**. The forecasting engine is the intellectual core of the system, responsible for:
    37→    37→
    38→    38→- **Demand Forecasting**: Predicting future demand for each SKU using multiple statistical models
    39→    39→- **Inventory Optimization**: Calculating optimal order quantities, safety stock levels, and reorder points
    40→    40→- **Order Trigger Timing**: Determining the exact date an order must be placed to avoid stockout, accounting for BD–China supply chain lead times and Chinese New Year (CNY) shutdowns
    41→    41→- **Consensus Planning**: Combining quantitative models with qualitative intelligence (marketing promos, sales field input) into a single agreed-upon forecast
    42→    42→- **Continuous Calibration**: Monitoring forecast accuracy and auto-recalibrating models when error thresholds are breached
    43→    43→
    44→    44→The forecasting engine runs as a **Python FastAPI microservice** alongside the primary **Laravel 11** backend, communicating via **Redis queues** and **PostgreSQL** shared database.

    61→    61→
    62→    62→#### Why a Separate Python Microservice?
    63→    63→
    64→    64→The forecasting engine is isolated from the Laravel monolith for the following reasons:
    65→    65→
    66→    66→1. **Library Ecosystem**: Prophet (Facebook's time-series library), statsmodels (regression/seasonal decomposition), scikit-learn (metrics, EOQ/SS calculations), numpy/pandas (data manipulation) — all Python-native with no PHP equivalents of comparable quality
    67→    67→2. **Computational Isolation**: Forecasting is CPU-intensive; isolating it prevents blocking Laravel's web-serving workers
    68→    68→3. **Independent Scaling**: Python workers can be scaled horizontally (add more Docker containers) independently of the Laravel app
    69→    69→4. **Polyglot Architecture**: Laravel excels at web framework concerns (auth, routing, ORM, queue management); Python excels at numerical computation — use each for its strength

   288→   288→## Section 2: Mathematical Models — Detailed Specification
   289→   289→
   290→   290→### 2.1 Model 1: Multi-Linear Regression (Baseline Forecast)
   291→   291→
   292→   292→#### Purpose
   293→   293→
   294→   294→Provides a **baseline demand estimate** based on price and promotional activity. This model answers: "Given the current price and promotional intensity, what is the expected demand?"
   295→   295→
   296→   296→#### Formula
   297→   297→
   298→   298→$$D(F) = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times \text{Price} + \beta_2 \times \text{PromoIndex}$$
   299→   299→
   300→   300→| Variable | Symbol | Type | Description |
   301→   301→|----------|--------|------|-------------|
   302→   302→| Forecasted Demand | D(F) | Dependent | Units demanded in the forecast period |
   303→   303→| Intercept | β₀ | Coefficient | Base demand when Price = 0 and PromoIndex = 0 |
   304→   304→| Price Coefficient | β₁ | Coefficient | Price elasticity — change in demand per 1 BDT price increase (typically negative) |
   305→   305→| Promo Coefficient | β₂ | Coefficient | Promo impact — change in demand per 0.1 promo_index increase (typically positive) |
   306→   306→| Price | Price | Independent | Product selling price in BDT |
   307→   307→| Promo Index | PromoIndex | Independent | Normalized promotional intensity: 0.0 = no promo, 1.0 = maximum promo activity |
   308→   308→
   309→   309→#### Beta Coefficients Interpretation
   310→   310→
   311→   311→| Coefficient | Interpretation | Example (BDT) | Business Meaning |
   312→   312→|-------------|---------------|---------------|------------------|
   313→   313→| β₀ | Base demand (intercept) | 150 units/month | Demand when price and promo are at zero — theoretical baseline |
   314→   314→| β₁ | Price elasticity | -2.5 units/BDT | Each 1 BDT price increase reduces demand by 2.5 units |
   315→   315→| β₂ | Promo impact | +30 units/0.1 index | Each 0.1 increase in promo_index adds 30 units of demand |
   316→   316→
   317→   317→#### Implementation
   318→   318→
   319→   319→```python
   320→   320→# app/models/regression_model.py
   321→   321→
   322→   322→import numpy as np
   323→   323→import pandas as pd
   324→   324→from statsmodels.regression.linear_model import OLS
   325→   325→from statsmodels.tools.tools import add_constant
   326→   326→from app.utils.outlier_detection import remove_sigma_outliers
   327→   327→
   328→   328→
   329→   329→class RegressionModel:
   330→   330→    """
   331→   331→    Multi-Linear Regression baseline forecast model.
   332→   332→
   333→   333→    D(F) = β₀ + β₁(Price) + β₂(Promo)
   334→   334→    """
   335→   335→
   336→   336→    def __init__(self, config: dict):
   337→   337→        self.regression_window_months = config.get("regression_window_months", 36)
   338→   338→        self.min_data_points = config.get("min_data_points", 12)
   339→   339→        self.outlier_sigma_threshold = config.get("outlier_sigma_threshold", 3.0)
   340→   340→
   341→   341→    def fit(self, df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
   342→   342→        """
   343→   343→        Fit the OLS regression model on historical sales data.
   344→   344→
   345→   345→        Args:
   346→   346→            df: DataFrame with columns [date, qty_sold, price, promo_index]
   347→   347→                Must have at least self.min_data_points rows.
   348→   348→
   349→   349→        Returns:
   350→   350→            Dictionary with coefficients, R², p-values, and model metadata.
   351→   351→
   352→   352→        Raises:
   353→   353→            ValueError: If insufficient data points after outlier removal.
   354→   354→        """
   355→   355→        # Validate minimum data points
   356→   356→        if len(df) < self.min_data_points:
   357→   357→            raise ValueError(
   358→   358→                f"Insufficient data: {len(df)} rows, "
   359→   359→                f"minimum required: {self.min_data_points}"
   360→   360→            )
   361→   361→
   362→   362→        # Remove outliers beyond sigma threshold
   363→   363→        df_clean = remove_sigma_outliers(
   364→   364→            df,
   365→   365→            column="qty_sold",
   366→   366→            sigma_threshold=self.outlier_sigma_threshold,
   367→   367→        )
   368→   368→
   369→   369→        if len(df_clean) < self.min_data_points:
   370→   370→            raise ValueError(
   371→   371→                f"Insufficient data after outlier removal: {len(df_clean)} rows, "
   372→   372→                f"minimum required: {self.min_data_points}"
   373→   373→            )
   374→   374→
   375→   375→        # Prepare feature matrix X and target y
   376→   376→        X = df_clean[["price", "promo_index"]].values
   377→   377→        X = add_constant(X)  # Adds β₀ intercept column
   378→   378→        y = df_clean["qty_sold"].values
   379→   379→
   380→   380→        # Fit OLS model
   381→   381→        model = OLS(y, X).fit()
   382→   382→
   383→   383→        return {
   384→   384→            "beta_0": float(model.params[0]),       # Intercept
   385→   385→            "beta_1": float(model.params[1]),       # Price coefficient
   386→   386→            "beta_2": float(model.params[2]),       # Promo coefficient
   387→   387→            "r_squared": float(model.rsquared),     # Model fit
   388→   388→            "adj_r_squared": float(model.rsquared_adj),
   389→   389→            "p_value_price": float(model.pvalues[1]),
   390→   390→            "p_value_promo": float(model.pvalues[2]),
   391→   391→            "f_statistic": float(model.fvalue),
   392→   392→            "f_pvalue": float(model.f_pvalue),
   393→   393→            "std_err_beta_0": float(model.bse[0]),
   394→   394→            "std_err_beta_1": float(model.bse[1]),
   395→   395→            "std_err_beta_2": float(model.bse[2]),
   396→   396→            "data_points_used": len(df_clean),
   397→   397→            "outliers_removed": len(df) - len(df_clean),
   398→   398→            "aic": float(model.aic),
   399→   399→            "bic": float(model.bic),
   400→   400→        }
   401→   401→
   402→   402→    def predict(self, coefficients: dict, price: float, promo_index: float) -> float:
   403→   403→        """
   404→   404→        Generate a demand forecast using fitted coefficients.
   405→   405→
   406→   406→        Args:
   407→   407→            coefficients: Dict with beta_0, beta_1, beta_2 from fit()
   408→   408→            price: Current/future product price in BDT
   409→   409→            promo_index: Promo intensity 0.0-1.0
   410→   410→
   411→   411→        Returns:
   412→   412→            Forecasted demand in units (float). Caller should round/ceil as needed.
   413→   413→        """
   414→   414→        forecast = (
   415→   415→            coefficients["beta_0"]
   416→   416→            + coefficients["beta_1"] * price
   417→   417→            + coefficients["beta_2"] * promo_index
   418→   418→        )
   419→   419→        return max(0.0, forecast)  # Demand cannot be negative
   420→   420→```
   421→   421→
   422→   422→#### Statistical Validation Checks
   423→   423→
   424→   424→| Check | Criterion | Action if Failed |
   425→   425→|-------|-----------|-----------------|
   426→   426→| R² goodness-of-fit | R² ≥ 0.3 | Log warning; model may be unreliable for this SKU |
   427→   427→| Price coefficient sign | β₁ < 0 | Log warning if positive (Giffen good — unusual for motorcycle parts) |
   428→   428→| Promo coefficient sign | β₂ > 0 | Log warning if negative (promo reduces demand — data quality issue) |
   429→   429→| F-test significance | F p-value < 0.05 | Flag model as statistically insignificant |
   430→   430→| Multicollinearity (VIF) | VIF < 5 for all predictors | If VIF ≥ 5, price and promo may be confounded |
   431→   431→| Residual normality | Shapiro-Wilk p > 0.05 | If non-normal, confidence intervals may be unreliable |
   432→   432→
   433→   433→#### Configuration Per Tenant
   434→   434→
   435→   435→| Parameter | Default | Valid Range | Description |
   436→   436→|-----------|---------|------------|-------------|
   437→   437→| `regression_window_months` | 36 | 12–60 | Number of months of historical data to use |
   438→   438→| `min_data_points` | 12 | 6–24 | Minimum data points required to fit the model |
   439→   439→| `outlier_sigma_threshold` | 3.0 | 2.0–5.0 | Data points beyond this σ are excluded |

   443→   443→### 2.2 Model 2: Prophet (Seasonal Forecast — Primary Model)
   444→   444→
   445→   445→#### Why Prophet Over Simple Regression
   446→   446→
   447→   447→| Capability | Prophet | Linear Regression | Exponential Smoothing |
   448→   448→|-----------|---------|-------------------|----------------------|
   449→   449→| Automatic seasonality detection | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ (must specify period) |
   450→   450→| Holiday effects | ✅ Built-in | ❌ | ❌ |
   451→   451→| Missing data handling | ✅ | ❌ (requires complete) | ⚠️ Partial |
   452→   452→| Outlier robustness | ✅ | ❌ (skews OLS) | ❌ |
   453→   453→| Confidence intervals | ✅ Automatic | ⚠️ (assumes normality) | ⚠️ |
   454→   454→| Trend changepoints | ✅ Automatic | ❌ (single trend) | ❌ |
   455→   455→| Multiplicative seasonality | ✅ | ❌ (additive only) | ⚠️ |
   456→   456→| Non-linear trends | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
   457→   457→
   458→   458→#### Prophet Configuration
   459→   459→
   460→   460→```python
   461→   461→# app/models/prophet_model.py
   462→   462→
   463→   463→from prophet import Prophet
   464→   464→from prophet.serialize import model_to_json, model_from_json
   465→   465→import pandas as pd
   466→   466→from datetime import datetime
   467→   467→from app.utils.bd_holidays import get_bd_holidays
   468→   468→from app.utils.bd_seasons import get_bd_custom_seasonalities
   469→   469→from app.utils.cny_calendar import get_cny_shutdown_window
   470→   470→
   471→   471→
   472→   472→class ProphetModel:
   473→   473→    """
   474→   474→    Prophet-based seasonal forecast model with BD-specific customizations.
   475→   475→    """
   476→   476→
   477→   477→    def __init__(self, config: dict):
   478→   478→        self.changepoint_prior_scale = config.get(
   479→   479→            "prophet_changepoint_prior_scale", 0.05
   480→   480→        )
   481→   481→        self.seasonality_prior_scale = config.get(
   482→   482→            "prophet_seasonality_prior_scale", 10.0
   483→   483→        )
   484→   484→        self.holidays_prior_scale = config.get(
   485→   485→            "prophet_holidays_prior_scale", 10.0
   486→   486→        )
   487→   487→        self.mcmc_samples = config.get("prophet_mcmc_samples", 0)
   488→   488→
   489→   489→    def build_model(
   490→   490→        self,
   491→   491→        year: int,
   492→   492→        include_cny: bool = True,
   493→   493→    ) -> Prophet:
   494→   494→        """
   495→   495→        Build a Prophet model with BD-specific configuration.
   496→   496→
   497→   497→        Args:
   498→   498→            year: The forecast year (used to generate holiday dates)
   499→   499→            include_cny: Whether to include CNY as a supply holiday
   500→   500→
   501→   501→        Returns:
   502→   502→            Configured Prophet model instance (not yet fitted).
   503→   503→        """
   504→   504→        # Get BD holidays for the relevant year range
   505→   505→        holidays_df = get_bd_holidays(
   506→   506→            years=[year - 1, year, year + 1],
   507→   507→            include_cny_supply_holiday=include_cny,
   508→   508→        )
   509→   509→
   510→   510→        model = Prophet(
   511→   511→            # --- Core Configuration ---
   512→   512→            yearly_seasonality=True,            # BD has strong yearly seasonal patterns
   513→   513→            weekly_seasonality=False,           # Motorcycle parts are NOT weekly-cyclical
   514→   514→            daily_seasonality=False,            # Not relevant for monthly/weekly data
   515→   515→            seasonality_mode="multiplicative",  # Seasonal effects MULTIPLY baseline
   516→   516→
   517→   517→            # --- Prior Scales (control overfitting) ---
   518→   518→            changepoint_prior_scale=self.changepoint_prior_scale,  # 0.05 = conservative
   519→   519→            seasonality_prior_scale=self.seasonality_prior_scale,  # 10 = strong seasonal
   520→   520→            holidays_prior_scale=self.holidays_prior_scale,        # 10 = strong holiday effect
   521→   521→
   522→   522→            # --- Performance ---
   523→   523→            mcmc_samples=self.mcmc_samples,  # 0 = fast MAP estimation (no full Bayesian)
   524→   524→
   525→   525→            # --- Holidays ---
   526→   526→            holidays=holidays_df,
   527→   527→        )
   528→   528→
   529→   529→        # --- BD-Specific Custom Seasonalities ---
   530→   530→        # These capture patterns that Prophet's default yearly Fourier series
   531→   531→        # may not adequately represent for the BD climate cycle.
   532→   532→
   533→   533→        # Winter Season (Nov-Feb): 4-month period
   534→   534→        # Strong positive effect for cold-weather parts (jackets, fog lamps, chain lube)
   535→   535→        model.add_seasonality(
   536→   536→            name="bd_winter",
   537→   537→            period=365.25 / 3,       # ~121.75 days (4-month cycle within year)
   538→   538→            fourier_order=3,          # Enough to capture the Nov-Feb peak shape
   539→   539→            prior_scale=15.0,         # Strong winter effect
   540→   540→            condition_name="is_winter",
   541→   541→        )
   542→   542→
   543→   543→        # Monsoon Season (Jun-Sep): 4-month period
   544→   544→        # Negative effect for street parts, positive for off-road/mud-specific parts
   545→   545→        model.add_seasonality(
   546→   546→            name="bd_monsoon",
   547→   547→            period=365.25 / 3,       # ~121.75 days
   548→   548→            fourier_order=3,
   549→   549→            prior_scale=12.0,
   550→   550→            condition_name="is_monsoon",
   551→   551→        )
   552→   552→
   553→   553→        # Pre-Winter Spike (October): transition month
   554→   554→        # Dealers pre-order before winter; brief demand surge
   555→   555→        model.add_seasonality(
   556→   556→            name="bd_pre_winter",
   557→   557→            period=365.25,           # Annual cycle
   558→   558→            fourier_order=2,         # Simpler shape — single spike
   559→   559→            prior_scale=8.0,
   560→   560→            condition_name="is_pre_winter",
   561→   561→        )
   562→   562→
   563→   563→        return model
   564→   564→
   565→   565→    def fit_predict(
   566→   566→        self,
   567→   567→        df: pd.DataFrame,
   568→   568→        forecast_horizon_days: int = 180,
   569→   569→        product_category: str = "general",
   570→   570→    ) -> dict:
   571→   571→        """
   572→   572→        Fit Prophet model and generate forecast.
   573→   573→
   574→   574→        Args:
   575→   575→            df: DataFrame with columns [ds (datetime), y (float qty_sold)]
   576→   576→                Must be sorted by ds ascending.
   577→   577→            forecast_horizon_days: Number of days to forecast ahead.
   578→   578→            product_category: Used to select seasonality condition columns
   579→   579→                              (e.g., 'cold_weather', 'off_road', 'street')
   580→   580→
   581→   581→        Returns:
   582→   582→            Dictionary with forecast values, confidence intervals, and components.
   583→   583→        """
   584→   584→        # Validate input
   585→   585→        if len(df) < 24:
   586→   586→            raise ValueError(
   587→   587→                f"Prophet requires at least 24 months of data; got {len(df)} rows"
   588→   588→            )
   589→   589→
   590→   590→        # Add seasonal condition columns based on BD calendar
   591→   591→        df = self._add_season_conditions(df, product_category)
   592→   592→
   593→   593→        # Build and fit model
   594→   594→        forecast_year = df["ds"].max().year
   595→   595→        model = self.build_model(year=forecast_year)
   596→   596→        model.fit(df)
   597→   597→
   598→   598→        # Generate future dataframe
   599→   599→        future = model.make_future_dataframe(
   600→   600→            periods=forecast_horizon_days,
   601→   601→            freq="D",
   602→   602→        )
   603→   603→        future = self._add_season_conditions(future, product_category)
   604→   604→
   605→   605→        # Make predictions
   606→   606→        forecast = model.predict(future)
   607→   607→
   608→   608→        # Extract results
   609→   609→        results = {
   610→   610→            "forecast": forecast[["ds", "yhat", "yhat_lower", "yhat_upper"]].to_dict(
   611→   611→                orient="records"
   612→   612→            ),
   613→   613→            "components": {
   614→   614→                "trend": forecast["trend"].tolist(),
   615→   615→                "yearly": forecast.get("yearly", pd.Series()).tolist(),
   616→   616→                "bd_winter": forecast.get("bd_winter", pd.Series()).tolist(),
   617→   617→                "bd_monsoon": forecast.get("bd_monsoon", pd.Series()).tolist(),
   618→   618→                "bd_pre_winter": forecast.get("bd_pre_winter", pd.Series()).tolist(),
   619→   619→                "holidays": forecast.get("holidays", pd.Series()).tolist(),
   620→   620→            },
   621→   621→            "model_serialized": model_to_json(model),  # For caching/reuse
   622→   622→        }
   623→   623→
   624→   624→        return results
   625→   625→
   626→   626→    def _add_season_conditions(
   627→   627→        self, df: pd.DataFrame, product_category: str
   628→   628→    ) -> pd.DataFrame:
   629→   629→        """
   630→   630→        Add boolean condition columns for custom seasonalities.
   631→   631→
   632→   632→        These columns tell Prophet when each custom seasonality is "active".
   633→   633→        The values depend on the product category because monsoon has OPPOSITE
   634→   634→        effects for different product types.
   635→   635→        """
   636→   636→        df = df.copy()
   637→   637→
   638→   638→        month = df["ds"].dt.month
   639→   639→
   640→   640→        # Winter: active Nov(11), Dec(12), Jan(1), Feb(2)
   641→   641→        df["is_winter"] = month.isin([11, 12, 1, 2]).astype(int)
   642→   642→
   643→   643→        # Monsoon: active Jun(6), Jul(7), Aug(8), Sep(9)
   644→   644→        # For cold_weather / general parts: monsoon REDUCES demand
   645→   645→        # For off_road / mud parts: monsoon INCREASES demand
   646→   646→        if product_category in ("off_road", "mud_chain", "off_road_tire"):
   647→   647→            df["is_monsoon"] = month.isin([6, 7, 8, 9]).astype(int)
   648→   648→        else:
   649→   649→            df["is_monsoon"] = month.isin([6, 7, 8, 9]).astype(int)
   650→   650→
   651→   651→        # Pre-Winter: active Oct(10) only
   652→   652→        df["is_pre_winter"] = (month == 10).astype(int)
   653→   653→
   654→   654→        return df
   655→   655→```
   656→   656→
   657→   657→#### BD-Specific Custom Seasonalities — Detail
   658→   658→
   659→   659→| Seasonality Name | Period | Fourier Order | Active Months | Effect Direction | Affected Products |
   660→   660→|-----------------|--------|---------------|---------------|-----------------|-------------------|
   661→   661→| `bd_winter` | 365.25/3 ≈ 121.75 days | 3 | Nov, Dec, Jan, Feb | **Positive** (+30-60% demand lift) | Cold-weather parts: fog lamps, chain lubricant, winter jackets, handlebar grips, heated grips, engine covers |
   662→   662→| `bd_monsoon` | 365.25/3 ≈ 121.75 days | 3 | Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep | **Negative** for street parts (-20-40%) / **Positive** for off-road (+15-30%) | Street: tires (slick), visors, fairings. Off-road: knobby tires, mud chains, splash guards |

   749→   749→                        "upper_window": 0,
   750→   750→                    })

   764→   764→    suppliers during this window. We model this as a Prophet holiday
   765→   765→    so the order trigger logic can account for it.

   767→   767→    from app.utils.cny_calendar import get_cny_shutdown_window

   774→   774→                "holiday": "CNY_Supply_Shutdown",

   776→   776→                "lower_window": 0,
   777→   777→                "upper_window": 0,
   778→   778→            })
   779→   779→    return records

   784→   784→```python
   785→   785→# app/utils/cny_calendar.py
   786→   786→
   787→   787→from datetime import date, timedelta
   788→   788→
   789→   789→
   790→   790→# Chinese New Year dates (the lunar new year falls on different Gregorian dates each year).
   791→   791→# CNY shutdown for Chinese factories/suppliers is typically:
   792→   792→# - Start: ~10 days before CNY day (factories wind down)
   793→   793→# - End: ~15-20 days after CNY day (factories restart, backlog clears)
   794→   794→# We use a conservative window: Jan 20 — Feb 20 (~30 days)
   795→   795→
   796→   796→CNY_DATES = {
   797→   797→    2024: date(2024, 2, 10),   # Year of the Dragon
   798→   798→    2025: date(2025, 1, 29),   # Year of the Snake
   799→   799→    2026: date(2026, 2, 17),   # Year of the Horse
   800→   800→    2027: date(2027, 2, 6),    # Year of the Goat
   801→   801→    2028: date(2028, 1, 26),   # Year of the Monkey
   802→   802→    2029: date(2029, 2, 13),   # Year of the Rooster
   803→   803→    2030: date(2030, 2, 3),    # Year of the Dog
   804→   804→}

   806→   806→
   807→   807→def get_cny_shutdown_window(year: int) -> tuple[date, date]:
   808→   808→    """
   809→   809→    Calculate the CNY supply shutdown window for a given year.
   810→   810→
   811→   811→    Conservative window: January 20 to February 20.
   812→   812→    This covers factory shutdown, worker migration, and restart backlog.
   813→   813→
   814→   814→    Args:
   815→   815→        year: The Gregorian year.
   816→   816→
   817→   817→    Returns:
   818→   818→        Tuple of (shutdown_start_date, shutdown_end_date).
   819→   819→    """
   820→   820→    return (date(year, 1, 20), date(year, 2, 20))

   823→   823→def get_cny_buffer_days(year: int) -> int:
   824→   824→    """
   825→   825→    Additional buffer days to account for:
   826→   826→    - Post-CNY backlog at supplier (orders placed before CNY get priority)
   827→   827→    - Shipping congestion after CNY (all Chinese exporters ship simultaneously)
   828→   828→
   829→   829→    Returns:
   830→   830→        Buffer in days (typically 7-14 days).
   831→   831→    """
   832→   832→    return 10  # Conservative default

   835→   835→def does_date_fall_in_cny(target_date: date) -> bool:
   836→   836→    """
   837→   837→    Check if a given date falls within the CNY shutdown window.
   838→   838→    """
   839→   839→    year = target_date.year
   840→   840→    cny_start, cny_end = get_cny_shutdown_window(year)
   841→   841→    return cny_start <= target_date <= cny_end

   844→   844→def adjust_for_cny(
   845→   845→    proposed_order_date: date,
   846→   846→    year: int,
   847→   847→    strategy: str = "before",
   848→   848→) -> dict:
   849→   849→    """
   850→   850→    Adjust an order date to avoid the CNY shutdown window.
   851→   851→
   852→   852→    Args:
   853→   853→        proposed_order_date: The originally calculated order trigger date.
   854→   854→        year: The Gregorian year.
   855→   855→        strategy: "before" (order before CNY starts) or "after" (order after CNY ends).
   856→   856→
   857→   857→    Returns:
   858→   858→        Dictionary with adjusted date, CNY risk flag, and explanation.
   859→   859→    """
   860→   860→    cny_start, cny_end = get_cny_shutdown_window(year)
   861→   861→    buffer = get_cny_buffer_days(year)
   862→   862→
   863→   863→    if not (cny_start <= proposed_order_date <= cny_end):
   864→   864→        return {
   865→   865→            "adjusted_date": proposed_order_date,
   866→   866→            "cny_risk": False,
   867→   867→            "original_date": proposed_order_date,
   868→   868→            "explanation": "Order date does not fall in CNY window. No adjustment needed.",
   869→   869→        }
   870→   870→
   871→   871→    if strategy == "before":
   872→   872→        adjusted = cny_start - timedelta(days=buffer + 1)
   873→   873→        explanation = (
   874→   874→            f"Order date {proposed_order_date} falls in CNY shutdown "
   875→   875→            f"({cny_start} to {cny_end}). Adjusted to order BEFORE CNY: {adjusted}. "
   876→   876→            f"This avoids stockout but requires earlier cash outflow."
   877→   877→        )
   878→   878→    else:  # strategy == "after"
   879→   879→        adjusted = cny_end + timedelta(days=1)
   880→   880→        explanation = (
   881→   881→            f"Order date {proposed_order_date} falls in CNY shutdown "
   882→   882→            f"({cny_start} to {cny_end}). Adjusted to order AFTER CNY: {adjusted}. "
   883→   883→            f"WARNING: This may cause stockout if current stock is insufficient "
   884→   884→            f"to cover the extended lead time."
   885→   885→        )
   886→   886→
   887→   887→    return {
   888→   888→        "adjusted_date": adjusted,
   889→   889→        "cny_risk": True,
   890→   890→        "original_date": proposed_order_date,
   891→   891→        "cny_start": cny_start,
   892→   892→        "cny_end": cny_end,
   893→   893→        "strategy_used": strategy,
   894→   894→        "explanation": explanation,
   895→   895→    }
   896→   896→```

   899→   899→
   900→   900→### 2.3 Model 3: Exponential Smoothing
   901→   901→
   902→   902→#### Purpose
   903→   903→
   904→   904→Provides a **short-term, responsive forecast** that heavily weights recent observations. Used as a complement to Prophet — particularly useful for:
   905→   905→
   906→   906→- New products with limited history (< 2 years, where Prophet may be unreliable)
   907→   907→- Products with recent trend changes (Prophet's changepoint detection may lag)
   908→   908→- SKU-level short-term adjustments in the consensus forecast
   909→   909→
   910→   910→#### Formula (Simple Exponential Smoothing)
   911→   911→
   912→   912→$$F_{t+1} = \alpha \times A_t + (1 - \alpha) \times F_t$$
   913→   913→
   914→   914→Where:
   915→   915→- $F_{t+1}$: Forecast for the next period
   916→   916→- $A_t$: Actual demand in the current period
   917→   917→- $F_t$: Forecast for the current period
   918→   918→- $\alpha$: Smoothing constant (0 < α < 1)
   919→   919→
   920→   920→#### Alpha Selection Guide
   921→   921→
   922→   922→| Alpha Range | Use Case | Behavior | Recommended For |
   923→   923→|-------------|----------|----------|-----------------|
   924→   924→| 0.1 – 0.3 | Stable/mature products | Smooth, slow-moving forecast; heavily weights history | Established SKUs with 3+ years of stable demand |
   925→   925→| 0.3 – 0.5 | Normal products | Balanced response to recent changes | Most motorcycle parts |
   926→   926→| 0.5 – 0.8 | New/trending products | Quick response to recent changes; volatile | New SKUs, products with recent demand shifts |
   927→   927→| 0.8 – 0.9 | Very volatile/reactive | Almost tracks actuals directly; minimal smoothing | Discontinued SKUs being run down, extreme trend shifts |
   928→   928→
   929→   929→#### Implementation
   930→   930→
   931→   931→```python
   932→   932→# app/models/exp_smoothing_model.py
   933→   933→
   934→   934→import numpy as np
   935→   935→import pandas as pd
   936→   936→from sklearn.metrics import mean_absolute_percentage_error, mean_absolute_error
   937→   937→from typing import Optional
   938→   938→
   939→   939→
   940→   940→class ExponentialSmoothingModel:
   941→   941→    """
   942→   942→    Exponential Smoothing with auto-tune alpha selection.
   943→   943→    """
   944→   944→
   945→   945→    def __init__(self, config: dict):
   946→   946→        self.default_alpha = config.get("default_alpha", 0.3)
   947→   947→        self.auto_tune = config.get("exp_smoothing_auto_tune", True)
   948→   948→        self.auto_tune_step = config.get("exp_smoothing_auto_tune_step", 0.1)
   949→   949→
   950→   950→    def fit_predict(
   951→   951→        self,
   952→   952→        series: pd.Series,
   953→   953→        forecast_periods: int = 6,
   954→   954→        alpha: Optional[float] = None,
   955→   955→    ) -> dict:
   956→   956→        """
   957→   957→        Apply exponential smoothing and generate forecast.
   958→   958→
   959→   959→        Args:
   960→   960→            series: Time series of actual demand values (sorted chronologically).
   961→   961→            forecast_periods: Number of periods to forecast ahead.
   962→   962→            alpha: Smoothing constant. If None, uses auto-tune or default.
   963→   963→
   964→   964→        Returns:
   965→   965→            Dictionary with forecast, alpha used, and smoothed values.
   966→   966→        """
   967→   967→        if alpha is None:
   968→   968→            if self.auto_tune and len(series) >= 12:
   969→   969→                alpha = self._auto_tune_alpha(series)
   970→   970→            else:
   971→   971→                alpha = self.default_alpha
   972→   972→
   973→   973→        # Apply exponential smoothing
   974→   974→        smoothed = self._smooth(series.values, alpha)
   975→   975→
   976→   976→        # Forecast: for simple ES, the forecast is flat (last smoothed value)
   977→   977→        # This is appropriate for demand without strong trend/seasonality
   978→   978→        last_smoothed = smoothed[-1]
   979→   979→        forecast = np.full(forecast_periods, last_smoothed)
   980→   980→
   981→   981→        return {
   982→   982→            "forecast": forecast.tolist(),
   983→   983→            "alpha": alpha,
   984→   984→            "smoothed_values": smoothed.tolist(),
   985→   985→            "last_smoothed_value": float(last_smoothed),
   986→   986→            "auto_tuned": alpha != self.default_alpha,
   987→   987→        }
   988→   988→
   989→   989→    def _smooth(self, values: np.ndarray, alpha: float) -> np.ndarray:
   990→   990→        """
   991→   991→        Apply exponential smoothing to a 1D array.
   992→   992→
   993→   993→        F[0] = A[0]  (initialize with first actual)
   994→   994→        F[t] = alpha * A[t] + (1 - alpha) * F[t-1]
   995→   995→        """
   996→   996→        smoothed = np.zeros_like(values, dtype=float)
   997→   997→        smoothed[0] = values[0]
   998→   998→
   999→   999→        for t in range(1, len(values)):
  1000→  1000→            smoothed[t] = alpha * values[t] + (1 - alpha) * smoothed[t - 1]
  1001→  1001→
  1002→  1002→        return smoothed
  1003→  1003→
  1004→  1004→    def _auto_tune_alpha(self, series: pd.Series) -> float:
  1005→  1005→        """
  1006→  1006→        Auto-tune alpha by testing values from 0.1 to 0.9 and selecting
  1007→  1007→        the value that produces the lowest MAPE on backtest.
  1008→  1008→
  1009→  1009→        Backtest approach:
  1010→  1010→        - Train on first 80% of data
  1011→  1011→        - Test on last 20%
  1012→  1012→        - Evaluate MAPE on test set
  1013→  1013→
  1014→  1014→        Args:
  1015→  1015→            series: Full time series of actual demand.
  1016→  1016→
  1017→  1017→        Returns:
  1018→  1018→            Best alpha value.
  1019→  1019→        """
  1020→  1020→        values = series.values
  1021→  1021→        split_idx = int(len(values) * 0.8)
  1022→  1022→        train = values[:split_idx]
  1023→  1023→        test = values[split_idx:]
  1024→  1024→
  1025→  1025→        best_alpha = self.default_alpha
  1026→  1026→        best_mape = float("inf")
  1027→  1027→
  1028→  1028→        alphas = np.arange(0.1, 1.0, self.auto_tune_step)
  1029→  1029→
  1030→  1030→        for alpha in alphas:
  1031→  1031→            # Smooth on training data
  1032→  1032→            smoothed = self._smooth(train, alpha)
  1033→  1033→
  1034→  1034→            # Forecast (flat) from last smoothed value
  1035→  1035→            forecast_values = np.full(len(test), smoothed[-1])
  1036→  1036→
  1037→  1037→            # Calculate MAPE on test set
  1038→  1038→            try:
  1039→  1039→                mape = mean_absolute_percentage_error(test, forecast_values)
  1040→  1040→            except (ZeroDivisionError, ValueError):
  1041→  1041→                continue
  1042→  1042→
  1043→  1043→            if mape < best_mape:
  1044→  1044→                best_mape = mape
  1045→  1045→                best_alpha = round(float(alpha), 2)
  1046→  1046→
  1047→  1047→        return best_alpha
  1048→  1048→```
  1049→  1049→
  1050→  1050→#### Configuration Per Tenant
  1051→  1051→
  1052→  1052→| Parameter | Default | Valid Range | Description |
  1053→  1053→|-----------|---------|------------|-------------|
  1054→  1054→| `default_alpha` | 0.3 | 0.05–0.95 | Default smoothing constant |
  1055→  1055→| `exp_smoothing_auto_tune` | True | True/False | Whether to auto-tune alpha via backtest |
  1056→  1056→| `exp_smoothing_auto_tune_step` | 0.1 | 0.01–0.2 | Step size for alpha grid search |

  1059→  1059→
  1060→  1060→### 2.4 Model 4: Economic Order Quantity (EOQ)
  1061→  1061→
  1062→  1062→#### Purpose
  1063→  1063→
  1064→  1064→Determines the **optimal order quantity** that minimizes total inventory cost (ordering cost + holding cost). This is the foundational inventory optimization formula.
  1065→  1065→
  1066→  1066→#### Formula
  1067→  1067→
  1068→  1068→$$EOQ = \sqrt{\frac{2KD}{h}}$$
  1069→  1069→
  1070→  1070→| Variable | Symbol | Unit | Source | Description |
  1071→  1071→|----------|--------|------|--------|-------------|
  1072→  1072→| Ordering cost | K | BDT/order | Tenant config | Fixed cost per purchase order (paperwork, communication, inspection) |
  1073→  1073→| Annual demand | D | units/year | Prophet/Regression | Forecasted annual demand for the SKU |
  1074→  1074→| Holding cost | h | BDT/unit/year | Computed | Annual cost to hold one unit in inventory |
  1075→  1075→
  1076→  1076→**Holding cost derivation**:
  1077→  1077→
  1078→  1078→$$h = \text{unit\_cost\_bdt} \times \text{holding\_cost\_pct}$$
  1079→  1079→
  1080→  1080→| Parameter | Default | Description |
  1081→  1081→|-----------|---------|-------------|
  1082→  1082→| unit_cost_bdt | Product-specific | Purchase price per unit from supplier (in BDT) |
  1083→  1083→| holding_cost_pct | 0.20 (20%) | Tenant configurable. Covers: warehouse space (5%), insurance (2%), obsolescence risk (8%), capital opportunity cost (5%) |
  1084→  1084→
  1085→  1085→#### Implementation
  1086→  1086→
  1087→  1087→```python
  1088→  1088→# app/models/eoq_model.py
  1089→  1089→
  1090→  1090→import math
  1091→  1091→from typing import Optional
  1092→  1092→
  1093→  1093→
  1094→  1094→class EOQModel:
  1095→  1095→    """
  1096→  1096→    Economic Order Quantity calculator with BD-specific constraints.
  1097→  1097→    """
  1098→  1098→
  1099→  1099→    def __init__(self, config: dict):
  1100→  1100→        self.default_ordering_cost = config.get("default_ordering_cost", 500.0)   # BDT
  1101→  1101→        self.default_holding_cost_pct = config.get("default_holding_cost_pct", 0.20)
  1102→  1102→
  1103→  1103→    def calculate(
  1104→  1104→        self,
  1105→  1105→        annual_demand: float,
  1106→  1106→        unit_cost: float,
  1107→  1107→        ordering_cost: Optional[float] = None,
  1108→  1108→        holding_cost_pct: Optional[float] = None,
  1109→  1109→        max_stock_qty: Optional[int] = None,
  1110→  1110→        supplier_moq: Optional[int] = None,
  1111→  1111→        warehouse_capacity_remaining: Optional[int] = None,
  1112→  1112→    ) -> dict:
  1113→  1113→        """
  1114→  1114→        Calculate EOQ with all constraints applied.
  1115→  1115→
  1116→  1116→        Args:
  1117→  1117→            annual_demand: Forecasted annual demand (units/year) from Prophet/Regression.
  1118→  1118→            unit_cost: Purchase price per unit in BDT.
  1119→  1119→            ordering_cost: Cost per purchase order in BDT. Uses default if None.
  1120→  1120→            holding_cost_pct: Holding cost as fraction of unit_cost. Uses default if None.
  1121→  1121→            max_stock_qty: Maximum stock quantity for this product (warehouse slot limit).
  1122→  1122→            supplier_moq: Supplier Minimum Order Quantity.
  1123→  1123→            warehouse_capacity_remaining: Remaining warehouse capacity in units.
  1124→  1124→
  1125→  1125→        Returns:
  1126→  1126→            Dictionary with EOQ, constrained EOQ, and all intermediate values.
  1127→  1127→        """
  1128→  1128→        if ordering_cost is None:
  1129→  1129→            ordering_cost = self.default_ordering_cost
  1130→  1130→        if holding_cost_pct is None:
  1131→  1131→            holding_cost_pct = self.default_holding_cost_pct
  1132→  1132→
  1133→  1133→        # Calculate holding cost per unit per year
  1134→  1134→        holding_cost_per_unit = unit_cost * holding_cost_pct
  1135→  1135→
  1136→  1136→        # Standard EOQ formula: sqrt(2KD/h)
  1137→  1137→        eoq_unconstrained = math.sqrt(
  1138→  1138→            (2 * ordering_cost * annual_demand) / holding_cost_per_unit
  1139→  1139→        )
  1140→  1140→
  1141→  1141→        # Apply constraints
  1142→  1142→        eoq = eoq_unconstrained
  1143→  1143→        constraints_applied = []
  1144→  1144→
  1145→  1145→        # Constraint 1: EOQ must not exceed max_stock_qty
  1146→  1146→        if max_stock_qty is not None and eoq > max_stock_qty:
  1147→  1147→            eoq = float(max_stock_qty)
  1148→  1148→            constraints_applied.append(
  1149→  1149→                f"EOQ capped to max_stock_qty={max_stock_qty}"
  1150→  1150→            )
  1151→  1151→
  1152→  1152→        # Constraint 2: EOQ must not be less than supplier MOQ
  1153→  1153→        if supplier_moq is not None and eoq < supplier_moq:
  1154→  1154→            eoq = float(supplier_moq)
  1155→  1155→            constraints_applied.append(
  1156→  1156→                f"EOQ raised to supplier_moq={supplier_moq}"
  1157→  1157→            )
  1158→  1158→
  1159→  1159→        # Constraint 3: EOQ must consider remaining warehouse capacity
  1160→  1160→        if warehouse_capacity_remaining is not None and eoq > warehouse_capacity_remaining:
  1161→  1161→            eoq = float(warehouse_capacity_remaining)
  1162→  1162→            constraints_applied.append(
  1163→  1163→                f"EOQ capped to warehouse_capacity_remaining={warehouse_capacity_remaining}"
  1164→  1164→            )
  1165→  1165→
  1166→  1166→        # Calculate derived values
  1167→  1167→        # Number of orders per year
  1168→  1168→        orders_per_year = annual_demand / eoq if eoq > 0 else 0
  1169→  1169→
  1170→  1170→        # Order cycle time (days between orders)
  1171→  1171→        order_cycle_days = 365 / orders_per_year if orders_per_year > 0 else 0
  1172→  1172→
  1173→  1173→        # Total annual ordering cost
  1174→  1174→        total_ordering_cost = orders_per_year * ordering_cost
  1175→  1175→
  1176→  1176→        # Total annual holding cost
  1177→  1177→        total_holding_cost = (eoq / 2) * holding_cost_per_unit
  1178→  1178→
  1179→  1179→        # Total annual inventory cost (ordering + holding)
  1180→  1180→        total_inventory_cost = total_ordering_cost + total_holding_cost
  1181→  1181→
  1182→  1182→        return {
  1183→  1183→            "eoq": round(eoq),
  1184→  1184→            "eoq_unconstrained": round(eoq_unconstrained),
  1185→  1185→            "holding_cost_per_unit_bdt": round(holding_cost_per_unit, 2),
  1186→  1186→            "orders_per_year": round(orders_per_year, 2),
  1187→  1187→            "order_cycle_days": round(order_cycle_days, 1),
  1188→  1188→            "total_ordering_cost_bdt": round(total_ordering_cost, 2),
  1189→  1189→            "total_holding_cost_bdt": round(total_holding_cost, 2),
  1190→  1190→            "total_inventory_cost_bdt": round(total_inventory_cost, 2),
  1191→  1191→            "constraints_applied": constraints_applied,
  1192→  1192→            "inputs": {
  1193→  1193→                "annual_demand": annual_demand,
  1194→  1194→                "unit_cost_bdt": unit_cost,
  1195→  1195→                "ordering_cost_bdt": ordering_cost,
  1196→  1196→                "holding_cost_pct": holding_cost_pct,
  1197→  1197→                "max_stock_qty": max_stock_qty,
  1198→  1198→                "supplier_moq": supplier_moq,
  1199→  1199→                "warehouse_capacity_remaining": warehouse_capacity_remaining,
  1200→  1200→            },
  1201→  1201→        }
  1202→  1202→```
  1203→  1203→
  1204→  1204→#### EOQ Example Calculation (BDT)

  1207→  1207→|-------|-------|
  1208→  1208→| Annual demand (D) | 1,200 units/year |

  1227→  1227→### 2.5 Model 5: Safety Stock (Comprehensive)
  1228→  1228→
  1229→  1229→#### Purpose
  1230→  1230→
  1231→  1231→Safety stock is the **buffer inventory** held to protect against:
  1232→  1232→1. **Demand uncertainty** — actual demand exceeding forecast
  1233→  1233→2. **Lead time variability** — supplier delivering later than expected
  1234→  1234→3. **Supply chain disruptions** — CNY, political instability, port congestion
  1235→  1235→
  1236→  1236→This is critical for the BD–China supply chain where lead times are long (3-5 months) and variable.
  1237→  1237→
  1238→  1238→#### Formula
  1239→  1239→
  1240→  1240→$$SS = \frac{EOQ}{R} + (MAE \times \mu_t \times \sigma_{LT}) \times k$$
  1241→  1241→
  1242→  1242→| Variable | Symbol | Unit | Source | Description |
  1243→  1243→|----------|--------|------|--------|-------------|
  1244→  1244→| Economic Order Quantity | EOQ | units | Model 4 | Optimal order quantity |
  1245→  1245→| Review period | R | days | Tenant config | Time between inventory reviews (default: 10 days) |
  1246→  1246→| Mean Absolute Error | MAE | units | Backtest | Average forecast error from backtest |
  1247→  1247→| Mean lead time | μₜ | days | purchase_history | Average actual lead time from historical orders |
  1248→  1248→| Lead time std dev | σ_LT | days | purchase_history | Standard deviation of actual lead times |
  1249→  1249→| Safety factor | k | dimensionless | Service level | z-score for desired service level |
  1250→  1250→
  1251→  1251→#### Safety Factor (k) by Service Level
  1252→  1252→
  1253→  1253→| Service Level | k (z-score) | Use Case |
  1254→  1254→|---------------|-------------|----------|
  1255→  1255→| 90% | 1.28 | Low-criticality parts (decorative, optional accessories) |
  1256→  1256→| 95% | 1.65 | Standard parts (default for most motorcycle parts) |
  1257→  1257→| 97.5% | 1.96 | High-turnover parts (brake pads, spark plugs, oil filters) |
  1258→  1258→| 99% | 2.33 | Critical parts (brake assemblies, engine components — stockout = safety risk) |
  1259→  1259→| 99.9% | 3.09 | Life-critical parts (brake discs, steering components) |

  1300→  1300→#### σ_LT (Lead Time Variability) Calculation
  1301→  1301→
  1302→  1302→```python
  1303→  1303→# app/models/safety_stock_model.py
  1304→  1304→
  1305→  1305→import math
  1306→  1306→import numpy as np
  1307→  1307→from typing import Optional
  1308→  1308→
  1309→  1309→
  1310→  1310→class SafetyStockModel:
  1311→  1311→    """
  1312→  1312→    Safety Stock calculator with BD-China supply chain awareness.
  1313→  1313→    """
  1314→  1314→
  1315→  1315→    # Default lead time std devs when insufficient historical data
  1316→  1316→    DEFAULT_SIGMA_LT_SEA = 15.0   # days
  1317→  1317→    DEFAULT_SIGMA_LT_AIR = 5.0    # days
  1318→  1318→    MIN_ORDERS_FOR_SIGMA = 5      # minimum historical orders to compute σ_LT
  1319→  1319→
  1320→  1320→    # Service level to safety factor mapping
  1321→  1321→    SERVICE_LEVEL_FACTORS = {
  1322→  1322→        0.90: 1.28,
  1323→  1323→        0.95: 1.65,
  1324→  1324→        0.975: 1.96,
  1325→  1325→        0.99: 2.33,
  1326→  1326→        0.999: 3.09,
  1327→  1327→    }
  1328→  1328→
  1329→  1329→    def __init__(self, config: dict):
  1330→  1330→        self.default_review_period_days = config.get("review_period_days", 10)
  1331→  1331→        self.default_service_level = config.get("default_service_level", 0.95)
  1332→  1332→
  1333→  1333→    def calculate(
  1334→  1334→        self,
  1335→  1335→        eoq: float,
  1336→  1336→        mae: float,
  1337→  1337→        mean_lead_time_days: float,
  1338→  1338→        lead_time_values: Optional[list[float]] = None,
  1339→  1339→        shipment_mode: str = "sea",
  1340→  1340→        service_level: Optional[float] = None,
  1341→  1341→        review_period_days: Optional[int] = None,
  1342→  1342→    ) -> dict:
  1343→  1343→        """
  1344→  1344→        Calculate safety stock using the comprehensive formula.
  1345→  1345→
  1346→  1346→        SS = (EOQ / R) + (MAE × μₜ × σ_LT) × k
  1347→  1347→
  1348→  1348→        Args:
  1349→  1349→            eoq: Economic Order Quantity (from Model 4).
  1350→  1350→            mae: Mean Absolute Error of forecast model (from backtest).
  1351→  1351→            mean_lead_time_days: Average lead time in days (μₜ).
  1352→  1352→            lead_time_values: List of historical actual lead times (for σ_LT calculation).
  1353→  1353→            shipment_mode: "sea" or "air" — used for default σ_LT fallback.
  1354→  1354→            service_level: Desired service level (0.90-0.999).
  1355→  1355→            review_period_days: Review period R in days.
  1356→  1356→
  1357→  1357→        Returns:
  1358→  1358→            Dictionary with safety stock, reorder point, and all intermediate values.
  1359→  1359→        """
  1360→  1360→        if service_level is None:
  1361→  1361→            service_level = self.default_service_level
  1362→  1362→        if review_period_days is None:
  1363→  1363→            review_period_days = self.default_review_period_days
  1364→  1364→
  1365→  1365→        # Calculate σ_LT
  1366→  1366→        sigma_lt = self._calculate_sigma_lt(
  1367→  1367→            lead_time_values=lead_time_values,
  1368→  1368→            shipment_mode=shipment_mode,
  1369→  1369→        )
  1370→  1370→
  1371→  1371→        # Get safety factor k
  1372→  1372→        k = self._get_safety_factor(service_level)
  1373→  1373→
  1374→  1374→        # Calculate safety stock components
  1375→  1375→        # Component 1: EOQ / R (cycle stock coverage during review period)
  1376→  1376→        component_cycle = eoq / review_period_days
  1377→  1377→
  1378→  1378→        # Component 2: (MAE × μₜ × σ_LT) × k (demand + lead time uncertainty)
  1379→  1379→        component_uncertainty = (mae * mean_lead_time_days * sigma_lt) * k
  1380→  1380→
  1381→  1381→        # Total safety stock
  1382→  1382→        safety_stock = component_cycle + component_uncertainty
  1383→  1383→
  1384→  1384→        # Calculate reorder point
  1385→  1385→        # ROP = (daily_demand × lead_time) + safety_stock
  1386→  1386→        # We derive daily_demand from EOQ and review period
  1387→  1387→        daily_demand = eoq / review_period_days
  1388→  1388→        reorder_point = (daily_demand * mean_lead_time_days) + safety_stock
  1389→  1389→
  1390→  1390→        return {
  1391→  1391→            "safety_stock": round(safety_stock),
  1392→  1392→            "reorder_point": round(reorder_point),
  1393→  1393→            "component_cycle_stock": round(component_cycle, 2),
  1394→  1394→            "component_uncertainty": round(component_uncertainty, 2),
  1395→  1395→            "sigma_lt_days": round(sigma_lt, 2),
  1396→  1396→            "safety_factor_k": k,
  1397→  1397→            "service_level": service_level,
  1398→  1398→            "daily_demand": round(daily_demand, 2),
  1399→  1399→            "lead_time_days": mean_lead_time_days,
  1400→  1400→            "inputs": {
  1401→  1401→                "eoq": eoq,
  1402→  1402→                "mae": mae,
  1403→  1403→                "mean_lead_time_days": mean_lead_time_days,
  1404→  1404→                "review_period_days": review_period_days,
  1405→  1405→                "shipment_mode": shipment_mode,
  1406→  1406→            },
  1407→  1407→        }
  1408→  1408→
  1409→  1409→    def _calculate_sigma_lt(
  1410→  1410→        self,
  1411→  1411→        lead_time_values: Optional[list[float]],
  1412→  1412→        shipment_mode: str,
  1413→  1413→    ) -> float:
  1414→  1414→        """
  1415→  1415→        Calculate standard deviation of lead time.
  1416→  1416→
  1417→  1417→        If we have sufficient historical data (≥ 5 orders), compute from actuals.
  1418→  1418→        Otherwise, use defaults based on shipment mode.
  1419→  1419→        """
  1420→  1420→        if lead_time_values and len(lead_time_values) >= self.MIN_ORDERS_FOR_SIGMA:
  1421→  1421→            return float(np.std(lead_time_values, ddof=1))  # Sample std dev
  1422→  1422→
  1423→  1423→        # Fallback defaults
  1424→  1424→        if shipment_mode == "air":
  1425→  1425→            return self.DEFAULT_SIGMA_LT_AIR
  1426→  1426→        return self.DEFAULT_SIGMA_LT_SEA
  1427→  1427→
  1428→  1428→    def _get_safety_factor(self, service_level: float) -> float:
  1429→  1429→        """
  1430→  1430→        Map service level to safety factor (z-score).
  1431→  1431→        Supports exact matches and linear interpolation between defined levels.
  1432→  1432→        """
  1433→  1433→        if service_level in self.SERVICE_LEVEL_FACTORS:
  1434→  1434→            return self.SERVICE_LEVEL_FACTORS[service_level]
  1435→  1435→
  1436→  1436→        # Linear interpolation between the two nearest defined levels
  1437→  1437→        levels = sorted(self.SERVICE_LEVEL_FACTORS.keys())
  1438→  1438→        for i in range(len(levels) - 1):
  1439→  1439→            if levels[i] <= service_level <= levels[i + 1]:
  1440→  1440→                ratio = (service_level - levels[i]) / (levels[i + 1] - levels[i])
  1441→  1441→                return (
  1442→  1442→                    self.SERVICE_LEVEL_FACTORS[levels[i]]
  1443→  1443→                    + ratio * (
  1444→  1444→                        self.SERVICE_LEVEL_FACTORS[levels[i + 1]]
  1445→  1445→                        - self.SERVICE_LEVEL_FACTORS[levels[i]]
  1446→  1446→                    )
  1447→  1447→                )
  1448→  1448→
  1449→  1449→        # Out of range — clamp
  1450→  1450→        if service_level < levels[0]:
  1451→  1451→            return self.SERVICE_LEVEL_FACTORS[levels[0]]
  1452→  1452→        return self.SERVICE_LEVEL_FACTORS[levels[-1]]
  1453→  1453→```
  1454→  1454→
  1455→  1455→#### Safety Stock Example Calculation
  1456→  1456→
  1457→  1457→| Input | Value |
  1458→  1458→|-------|-------|
  1459→  1459→| EOQ | 200 units |
  1460→  1460→| Review period (R) | 10 days |
  1461→  1461→| MAE (from backtest) | 12 units |
  1462→  1462→| Mean lead time (μₜ) | 152 days (sea) |
  1463→  1463→| σ_LT | 15 days |
  1464→  1464→| Service level | 95% → k = 1.65 |
  1465→  1465→
  1466→  1466→$$SS = \frac{200}{10} + (12 \times 152 \times 15) \times 1.65 = 20 + 45{,}144 = 45{,}164$$
  1467→  1467→
  1468→  1468→> **Note**: The `(MAE × μₜ × σ_LT)` term can produce very large values when lead times are long. In practice, the formula is often decomposed and the MAE is scaled to a daily rate. The production implementation includes a `normalize_mae_to_daily` flag (default: True) that divides MAE by 30 before the calculation, yielding more realistic safety stock values.

  1471→  1471→
  1472→  1472→### 2.6 Model 6: Order Trigger Date Calculator (THE CORE IP)
  1473→  1473→
  1474→  1474→> **This is the most valuable algorithm in the TrimedCast system.**
  1475→  1475→>
  1476→  1476→> It answers the most critical question in the BD–China motorcycle parts supply chain:
  1477→  1477→> **"On what exact date must we place this order to ensure stock doesn't run out before the next shipment arrives?"**
  1478→  1478→>
  1479→  1479→> This is non-trivial because:
  1480→  1480→> - Lead times are 3-5 months (sea) or 3.5 months (air)
  1481→  1481→> - Chinese New Year creates a ~30-day supply blackout every year
  1482→  1482→> - Demand is seasonal (winter parts peak in Nov-Feb)
  1483→  1483→> - Missing the order window means stockout for months (no quick reorder possible)
  1484→  1484→
  1485→  1485→#### Algorithm — Step-by-Step
  1486→  1486→
  1487→  1487→```
  1488→  1488→┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  1489→  1489→│                   ORDER TRIGGER DATE CALCULATOR                         │
  1490→  1490→│                    Step-by-Step Algorithm                               │
  1491→  1491→└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  1492→  1492→
  1493→  1493→STEP 1: Calculate when stock will hit Reorder Point
  1494→  1494→──────────────────────────────────────────────────
  1495→  1495→  daily_consumption_rate = avg_demand_last_3_months / 90
  1496→  1496→  days_until_reorder = (qty_on_hand - reorder_point) / daily_consumption_rate
  1497→  1497→  reorder_hit_date = today + days_until_reorder
  1498→  1498→
  1499→  1499→  ⚠️  If qty_on_hand <= reorder_point → ORDER IMMEDIATELY (already below ROP)
  1500→  1500→
  1501→  1501→STEP 2: Calculate Total Lead Time
  1502→  1502→──────────────────────────────────
  1503→  1503→  if shipment_mode == 'sea':
  1504→  1504→      total_lt = mfg_days + sea_shipment_days + customs_days
  1505→  1505→  elif shipment_mode == 'air':
  1506→  1506→      total_lt = mfg_days + air_shipment_days + customs_days
  1507→  1507→
  1508→  1508→  Add CNY buffer if order falls in CNY window:
  1509→  1509→  if does_date_fall_in_cny(order_trigger_date):
  1510→  1510→      total_lt += cny_buffer_days  (default: 10 days)
  1511→  1511→
  1512→  1512→STEP 3: Calculate Order Trigger Date
  1513→  1513→──────────────────────────────────────
  1514→  1514→  order_trigger_date = reorder_hit_date - total_lt
  1515→  1515→
  1516→  1516→  ⚠️  If order_trigger_date < today → ORDER IMMEDIATELY (already late)
  1517→  1517→
  1518→  1518→STEP 4: Check CNY Risk
  1519→  1519→──────────────────────
  1520→  1520→  if order_trigger_date falls within CNY shutdown (Jan 20 — Feb 20):
  1521→  1521→      Option A: Order BEFORE CNY
  1522→  1522→          order_trigger_date = CNY_start - buffer_days
  1523→  1523→          → Avoids stockout but requires earlier cash outflow
  1524→  1524→      Option B: Order AFTER CNY
  1525→  1525→          order_trigger_date = CNY_end + 1
  1526→  1526→          → Delays order but risks stockout
  1527→  1527→
  1528→  1528→      RECOMMENDATION: Option A if cash flow permits
  1529→  1529→
  1530→  1530→STEP 5: Calculate Full Timeline
  1531→  1531→───────────────────────────────
  1532→  1532→  order_trigger_date          → Place order with supplier
  1533→  1533→  mfg_complete_date           → order_trigger_date + manufacturing_days
  1534→  1534→  ship_date                   → mfg_complete_date + 2 (packing/loading buffer)
  1535→  1535→  arrival_date                → ship_date + shipment_days
  1536→  1536→  customs_clearance_date      → arrival_date + customs_days
  1537→  1537→  available_for_sale_date     → customs_clearance_date + 1
  1538→  1538→
  1539→  1539→OUTPUT: Complete order timeline with every milestone date
  1540→  1540→```

  1607→  1607→            Dictionary with order_trigger_date, full timeline, CNY risk assessment.

  1609→  1609→        if today is None:
  1610→  1610→            today = date.today()
  1611→  1611→

  1618→  1618→            else:
  1619→  1619→                shipment_days = self.default_air_shipment_days

  1621→  1621→            if shipment_mode == "sea":
  1622→  1622→                customs_days = self.default_customs_days_sea
  1623→  1623→            else:
  1624→  1624→                customs_days = self.default_customs_days_air
  1625→  1625→

  1627→  1627→        if qty_on_hand <= reorder_point:
  1628→  1628→            # Already at or below ROP → ORDER IMMEDIATELY
  1629→  1629→            step1 = {
  1630→  1630→                "order_urgent": True,
  1631→  1631→                "days_until_reorder": 0,
  1632→  1632→                "reorder_hit_date": today,
  1633→  1633→                "message": "Stock is at or below reorder point. ORDER IMMEDIATELY.",
  1634→  1634→            }

  1636→  1636→        else:

  1638→  1638→                raise ValueError("daily_consumption_rate must be positive")
  1639→  1639→            days_until_reorder = (qty_on_hand - reorder_point) / daily_consumption_rate
  1640→  1640→            reorder_hit_date = today + timedelta(days=round(days_until_reorder))

  1642→  1642→                "order_urgent": False,
  1643→  1643→                "days_until_reorder": round(days_until_reorder, 1),
  1644→  1644→                "reorder_hit_date": reorder_hit_date,
  1645→  1645→                "message": f"Stock will hit ROP in {days_until_reorder:.1f} days.",
  1646→  1646→            }
  1647→  1647→

  1649→  1649→        total_lt = mfg_days + shipment_days + customs_days

  1662→  1662→
  1663→  1663→        # ─── STEP 4: Check CNY Risk ───
  1664→  1664→        cny_result = adjust_for_cny(
  1665→  1665→            proposed_order_date=order_trigger_date,
  1666→  1666→            year=order_trigger_date.year,
  1667→  1667→            strategy=cny_strategy,

  1669→  1669→

  1682→  1682→        )
  1683→  1683→
  1684→  1684→        return {

  1816→  1816→## Section 3: Consensus Forecast Logic
  1817→  1817→
  1818→  1818→### 3.1 Overview
  1819→  1819→
  1820→  1820→The Consensus Forecast is TrimedCast's approach to combining multiple quantitative models with qualitative human intelligence into a **Single Set of Numbers** — the final forecast that drives all purchasing and inventory decisions.
  1821→  1821→
  1822→  1822→### 3.2 Consensus Forecast Pipeline
  1823→  1823→
  1824→  1824→```
  1825→  1825→┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  1826→  1826→│                    CONSENSUS FORECAST PIPELINE                          │
  1827→  1827→│                                                                         │
  1828→  1828→│  Step 1: Prophet Seasonal Forecast (quantitative baseline)              │
  1829→  1829→│     ↓                                                                   │
  1830→  1830→│  Step 2: Apply BD Seasonal Weights (per-SKU seasonal multipliers)       │
  1831→  1831→│     ↓                                                                   │
  1832→  1832→│  Step 3: Apply Marketing Adjustments (promo_index → β₂ adjustment)      │
  1833→  1833→│     ↓                                                                   │
  1834→  1834→│  Step 4: Apply Sales Field Intelligence (manual overrides + audit)      │
  1835→  1835→│     ↓                                                                   │
  1836→  1836→│  Step 5: Consensus Forecast = "Single Set of Numbers"                   │
  1837→  1837→│                                                                         │
  1838→  1838→└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  1839→  1839→```
  1840→  1840→
  1841→  1841→### 3.3 Step-by-Step Detail
  1842→  1842→
  1843→  1843→#### Step 1: Prophet Seasonal Forecast (Quantitative Baseline)
  1844→  1844→
  1845→  1845→The Prophet model output serves as the starting point. This is the pure statistical forecast incorporating:
  1846→  1846→- Historical demand patterns
  1847→  1847→- BD seasonal effects (winter, monsoon, pre-winter)
  1848→  1848→- BD holiday effects (Eid, Puja, etc.)
  1849→  1849→- Trend and changepoints
  1850→  1850→
  1851→  1851→```
  1852→  1852→prophet_forecast[month] = yhat value from Prophet model
  1853→  1853→```
  1854→  1854→
  1855→  1855→#### Step 2: Apply BD Seasonal Weights
  1856→  1856→
  1857→  1857→Each SKU has seasonal weight multipliers defined by the tenant (with sensible defaults). These capture product-specific seasonal patterns that Prophet's general seasonality may not fully represent.
  1858→  1858→
  1859→  1859→| Month | Default Weight | Cold Weather Parts | Off-Road Parts | Street Parts |
  1860→  1860→|-------|---------------|--------------------|----------------|-------------|
  1861→  1861→| Jan | 1.2 | 1.8 | 0.8 | 0.7 |
  1862→  1862→| Feb | 1.1 | 1.5 | 0.8 | 0.8 |
  1863→  1863→| Mar | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0.9 | 1.0 |
  1864→  1864→| Apr | 1.0 | 0.7 | 0.9 | 1.1 |
  1865→  1865→| May | 0.9 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.0 |
  1866→  1866→| Jun | 0.8 | 0.3 | 1.3 | 0.7 |
  1867→  1867→| Jul | 0.8 | 0.3 | 1.4 | 0.6 |
  1868→  1868→| Aug | 0.8 | 0.3 | 1.3 | 0.7 |
  1869→  1869→| Sep | 0.9 | 0.4 | 1.2 | 0.8 |
  1870→  1870→| Oct | 1.1 | 0.8 | 1.0 | 1.1 |
  1871→  1871→| Nov | 1.2 | 1.6 | 0.9 | 1.0 |
  1872→  1872→| Dec | 1.3 | 2.0 | 0.8 | 0.8 |
  1873→  1873→
  1874→  1874→```
  1875→  1875→after_seasonal[month] = prophet_forecast[month] × seasonal_weight[month][sku_category]
  1876→  1876→```
  1877→  1877→
  1878→  1878→#### Step 3: Apply Marketing Adjustments
  1879→  1879→
  1880→  1880→The regression model's promo coefficient (β₂) is used to adjust the forecast based on planned promotional activity.
  1881→  1881→
  1882→  1882→```
  1883→  1883→promo_adjustment = β₂ × (planned_promo_index - baseline_promo_index)
  1884→  1884→after_marketing[month] = after_seasonal[month] + promo_adjustment
  1885→  1885→```
  1886→  1886→
  1887→  1887→Where:
  1888→  1888→- `planned_promo_index`: The marketing team's planned promo intensity for the forecast month (0.0 - 1.0)
  1889→  1889→- `baseline_promo_index`: The average promo_index from the historical training data
  1890→  1890→
  1891→  1891→#### Step 4: Apply Sales Field Intelligence
  1892→  1892→
  1893→  1893→Sales representatives who visit dealers daily have ground-level intelligence that statistical models cannot capture:
  1894→  1894→- A dealer is expanding to a second location (demand increase)
  1895→  1895→- A competitor's supply is disrupted (demand shift)
  1896→  1896→- A new government regulation is coming (demand spike or drop)
  1897→  1897→- A local event (rally, fair) will temporarily boost demand
  1898→  1898→
  1899→  1899→This is captured as a **manual override** with mandatory audit trail:
  1900→  1900→
  1901→  1901→```python
  1902→  1902→# Manual override schema
  1903→  1903→{
  1904→  1904→    "sku_id": "SKU-12345",
  1905→  1905→    "month": "2025-07",
  1906→  1906→    "override_qty": 500,          # Sales rep's estimate
  1907→  1907→    "reason_code": "COMPETITOR_OOS",  # From enum: COMPETITOR_OOS, NEW_DEALER, REGULATION, EVENT, OTHER
  1908→  1908→    "reason_text": "Main competitor (X Parts) has factory issue, expect demand shift",
  1909→  1909→    "submitted_by": "user_id_456",
  1910→  1910→    "submitted_at": "2025-06-15T10:30:00Z",
  1911→  1911→    "confidence_level": "medium",  # low, medium, high
  1912→  1912→}
  1913→  1913→```
  1914→  1914→
  1915→  1915→**Override blending logic**:
  1916→  1916→
  1917→  1917→| Confidence Level | Blend Weight | Formula |
  1918→  1918→|-----------------|-------------|---------|
  1919→  1919→| low | 0.2 | `consensus = 0.8 × statistical + 0.2 × override` |
  1920→  1920→| medium | 0.4 | `consensus = 0.6 × statistical + 0.4 × override` |
  1921→  1921→| high | 0.7 | `consensus = 0.3 × statistical + 0.7 × override` |
  1922→  1922→
  1923→  1923→If no override exists for a SKU-month, Step 4 is skipped and the statistical forecast is used as-is.
  1924→  1924→
  1925→  1925→#### Step 5: Final Consensus Forecast
  1926→  1926→
  1927→  1927→```
  1928→  1928→consensus_forecast[month] = after_marketing[month]  (if no override)
  1929→  1929→consensus_forecast[month] = blended_value            (if override exists)
  1930→  1930→```
  1931→  1931→
  1932→  1932→This is the **"Single Set of Numbers"** — the final forecast that:
  1933→  1933→- Drives EOQ and safety stock calculations
  1934→  1934→- Determines order trigger dates
  1935→  1935→- Feeds the dashboard and reports
  1936→  1936→- Is used in S&OP (Sales & Operations Planning) meetings
  1937→  1937→
